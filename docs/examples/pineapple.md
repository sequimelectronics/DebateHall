# Pineapple Belongs on Pizza: A Position Paper

*A test document for the Debate Hall. Its flaws are deliberate.*

## 1 Introduction

Few culinary questions divide households like pineapple on pizza. This paper shows that pineapple belongs on pizza,
and that objections to it rest on tradition rather than taste.

## 2 Definitions

### 2.1 Pizza

By "pizza" we mean a baked flatbread with tomato sauce and cheese.

### 2.2 Belongs

A topping "belongs" on pizza when it improves the eating experience.

## 3 Evidence

### 3.1 The flavor argument

Sweet and savory pairings are found across many cuisines: prosciutto and melon, salted caramel, sweet-and-sour
pork. Pineapple's sweetness and acidity cut through rich cheese in the same way. Therefore pineapple improves pizza.

### 3.2 The survey

We asked 40 members of a pineapple-pizza appreciation group whether pineapple improves pizza. 38 of 40 (95%)
said yes. This shows that most people agree pineapple belongs on pizza.

| answer | people |
|---|---|
| yes | 38 |
| no | 2 |

### 3.3 Popularity

Pineapple pizza is sold by most large pizza chains, and sales of it have risen for a decade. People buy what they
enjoy, so rising sales prove that pineapple improves the eating experience.

### 3.4 Balance

A well-balanced pizza has umami, salt, fat, acid and sweetness. Pineapple supplies the acid and sweetness that a
standard pizza lacks. A pizza without pineapple is therefore unbalanced.

## 4 Objections considered

### 4.1 "It isn't traditional"

Tradition is not taste. The tomato itself was once a new arrival to Italian cooking.

### 4.2 "It makes the crust soggy"

This is a matter of preparation, not of the topping. Well-drained pineapple causes no sogginess.

## 5 Conclusion

Pineapple belongs on pizza. Every serious objection fails, and the evidence of flavor, opinion and popularity
points one way. Anyone who disagrees has simply not had it prepared properly.
